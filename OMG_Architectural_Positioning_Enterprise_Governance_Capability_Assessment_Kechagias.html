Official OMG Positioning

OMG (OrchestrAI Model Governance) is positioned as a Governance Intelligence and Governance Operations Platform.

Its purpose is to establish governance state, accountability, evidence, traceability, governance continuity, reassessment, governance intelligence, and decision reconstruction across AI assets, models, agents, prompts, tools, and knowledge assets.

OMG is not intended to replace IAM platforms, SSO platforms, runtime enforcement engines, AI gateways, workflow engines, SIEMs, security platforms, execution platforms, control planes, or agent runtimes.

Instead, OMG is designed to operate alongside those technologies by providing governance requirements, governance decisions, governance evidence, accountability, governance continuity, and governance intelligence.

Section 2

OMG Architectural Positioning

Authority flows down into governance; governance flows down into execution. OMG occupies exactly one layer of that flow.

Governance Authority

Who is accountable for a decision. OMG captures this descriptively — GovernanceAuthorityProfile (types/index.ts:279-288) — and now re-derives whether that authority is current via authorityCurrencyEngine.ts. It does not verify the named person's real-world identity or standing.

Governance Operations

The day-to-day work of assessing, approving, evidencing, and reassessing. This is OMG's deepest and most verified capability area — assets, evidence, findings, corrective actions, certification, and the new engine layer all operate here.

Runtime Enforcement & Execution

Acting on a decision — blocking, pausing, executing, intercepting. Confirmed absent from OMG by direct inspection: no code anywhere in frontend/src or backend/src executes or intercepts a running process. The Kill Switch and Revalidation modules are 100% human-triggered recommendations.

Where OMG Creates Unique Value

What distinguishes these from a traditional GRC (Governance, Risk & Compliance) system.

Governance Continuity

Tracks whether a decision remains valid over time, not just whether it was made once.

vs. traditional GRC: most GRC tools record a point-in-time approval and stop; OMG continuously re-evaluates whether that approval still holds.

Reassessment

A 15-type trigger taxonomy with a working consequence pathway and a now-reachable human filing entry point.

vs. traditional GRC: reassessment is usually a manual calendar reminder; here it is a typed, structured event with a defined downstream effect.

Governance Journey

A real, aggregated view of an asset's path from registration through production and monitoring.

vs. traditional GRC: most GRC dashboards show current status only, not the journey that produced it.

Decision Reconstruction

Reconstructs the conditions under which a past decision was made, for comparison against current conditions.

vs. traditional GRC: few GRC systems can answer "what did we know when we approved this?" as a first-class query.

Evidence Traceability

Links evidence to the specific risk assessment, review, decision, and reauthorization it supports.

Five-Way Accountability

Every governed asset carries five distinct accountability roles at once — business owner, technical owner, risk owner, compliance owner, and approver (OwnershipAssignment, types/index.ts:44-50) — not a single "owner" field.

vs. traditional GRC: most systems track one owner per asset; OMG structurally requires five distinct accountable roles.

Governability Assessment

A single, composite, explainable verdict — Governable / Governable With Conditions / Review Required / Governance Attention Required / Not Governable — computed from evidence, authority, and admissibility together, not read off one field.

Authority Currency

Re-derives whether the named decision authority is still current, rather than trusting a static field indefinitely.

Evidence Sufficiency

Scores whether evidence is actually enough — availability, recency, completeness, relevance, and context-alignment — not merely present.

Admissibility Assessment

Diffs the conditions a decision was made under against current conditions, and recommends Continue, Escalate, Reauthorize, or Pause accordingly.

Governance Intelligence

A reasoning layer that composes multiple independent signals into one recommendation, advisory-only throughout.

Final Executive Conclusion

The honest verdict

OMG is best understood as a Governance Intelligence and Governance Operations Platform.

The next evolution of OMG is not becoming a runtime platform, but strengthening governability intelligence through authority currency, evidence sufficiency, admissibility assessment, governability status, governance signal intelligence, and governance continuity — while preserving a clear architectural separation between authority, governance, and execution.
