1 · Executive Summary

Evaluated against its own mission, not a borrowed one

Assessed on its own terms, OMG is a mature and coherent Governance Intelligence, Continuity, Configuration, and Evidence & Traceability platform. Its governance domain modeling — continuity, reassessment, evidence, findings, corrective actions, certification, readiness, the reasoning engine, Governance Studio, the Policy Engine — is real, deep, and internally consistent, verified directly against code. The current public deployment's seeded persona access and demo-grade login are not a security failure; they are the correct implementation of a stated design goal: letting evaluators explore every persona and governance workflow without deployment friction. Judged against that goal, the demonstration environment succeeds.

Where this assessment differs sharply from a conventional audit: most of what would read as "critical gaps" in an enterprise-readiness review — no built-in SSO, no runtime enforcement, no multi-tenancy, no AI-gateway execution interception — are not gaps at all once measured against what OMG says it is. OMG explicitly is not an IAM platform, not a runtime execution platform, not an AI gateway or broker, and not a workflow orchestration engine. Their absence is the correct, intentional shape of the product, with a clear delivery path already defined by the ODF model itself (Layer 3/4) for exactly the moment a real customer needs them.

2 · OMG Strategic Positioning

What OMG is, stated plainly, and verified against code

OMG is, and code confirms it

A Governance Intelligence Layer — real reasoning engines (conditions → policy violations → outcome ladder → recommended actions), all explainable, all advisory.

A Governance Continuity Platform — live, computed reauthorization status and a 13-type reassessment trigger taxonomy with real downstream consequences.

A Governance Configuration Platform — Governance Studio (enable/disable conditions, outcomes, action rules; author policies and compliance packs) is real, interactive, and RBAC-gated.

A Governance Evidence & Traceability Platform — Evidence Registry, Findings, Corrective Actions, Certification lifecycle, Decision Reconstruction, and the Governance Journey Explorer are all real and verified against seeded data.

A Governance Operating System(loosely) — in the sense of coordinating governance activity across 8 domains and ~106 pages through one consistent data and reasoning model.

OMG is not, and does not claim to be

A runtime execution platform — zero code touches a running agent, model, or tool process.

An AI gateway or broker — no execution-request interception point exists anywhere.

An IAM platform — no identity verification; RBAC is a governance-relevant permission model, not an identity system.

A SIEM platform — no telemetry ingestion, no live monitoring of external systems.

A workflow orchestration engine — each governance process (decision, certification, kill-switch) is its own bespoke, purpose-built logic, not instances of a generic engine, by design.

A cloud platform or production-ready SaaS offering — the current deployment is an intentional demonstration/evaluation environment.

Every capability marked absent later in this report is checked first against this list. If it belongs to the right-hand column, its absence is evidence OMG is correctly positioned — not evidence it is incomplete.
3 · OMG Scope Boundaries

The demonstration environment is a design decision, not a shortfall

The current public deployment's seeded, persona-based access is explicitly the correct implementation of a stated goal: allow evaluators to explore all personas, review governance workflows, inspect evidence, inspect continuity, inspect traceability, and validate capabilities — without deployment friction. A credentialed login, tenant provisioning, or SSO handshake in front of that experience would work directly against this goal, not toward it.

This reframes several previously-flagged items. Frictionless persona access, a single shared demo dataset, and the absence of tenant isolation are not defects to fix inside the evaluation environment — they are the mechanism by which the evaluation environment does its job. The scope boundary that matters is: which of these choices stay true once a real customer's production deployment is being built — and for those, the ODF delivery model below already defines exactly who does that work and when.

4 · Common vs. Configurable vs. ODF Extension vs. Customer Responsibility

The ODF four-layer delivery model, populated with verified capabilities

Only real, code-verified capabilities appear in Layers 1 and 2. Layers 3 and 4 describe where the remaining work correctly belongs — not what is missing from Layer 1.

1 Common OMG Platform — standard for every customer

Governance Continuity (reauthorization status) · Reassessment taxonomy + automatic state consequence · Governance Journey Explorer, Decision Reconstruction, Governance Story, Governance Value metrics · Evidence Registry, Findings, Corrective Actions, Certification issue/renew/revoke workflow · Governance Readiness (per-asset and portfolio-wide) · Executive Dashboards · Governance Reasoning Engine and its Human Decision Layer (Accept/Reject/Defer) · the RBAC action-key permission model · a dual-mode data architecture (localStorage evaluation mode / Postgres+NestJS production mode) with a working migration utility · the Kill Switch mechanism as a human-gated, audited control · the local Audit Trail.

2 Configurable OMG Platform — per-customer, no custom development

Governance Studio: enable/disable individual conditions, outcomes, and action rules; author and edit Policies and Policy Mappings; the Compliance Pack Builder (Regulatory Source → Requirement → Obligation → Control → Policy); Customer Governance Profile activation. This is genuinely real and interactive today — not a mockup.

3 ODF Implementation Extensions — delivered jointly during onboarding

Integrations (identity provider binding, monitoring/ITSM connectors, notification delivery to the customer's own tools) · production deployment engineering (CI/CD, IaC, environment provisioning) for a specific customer's environment · organization-specific governance process content authored into OMG's existing generic structures · regulatory interpretation for a customer's specific obligations · operating-model alignment (mapping OMG's roles onto the customer's real org chart) · multi-step, organization-specific approval chain configuration.

4 Customer-Specific Implementations — intentionally outside OMG core

Runtime enforcement and execution control · customer control planes and orchestration infrastructure · organization-specific authority structures beyond what OMG's descriptive profile fields capture · sector-specific approval models where they require infrastructure OMG doesn't own · the customer's own execution infrastructure and identity provider. These stay outside OMG core because building them would require OMG to become a fundamentally different kind of system (a runtime/IAM/orchestration platform) — not because they were overlooked.

A tiered (Ready/Partially Ready/Not Ready) system and a separate numeric-score system overlap in naming and concept — an internal coherence gap inside the Governance Intelligence Layer, worth resolving on its own merits.

10 · Final Strategic Assessment

The honest verdict

OMG is exactly what it claims to be: a governance intelligence, continuity, configuration, and evidence & traceability platform, currently and appropriately presented as a demonstration/evaluation environment. Its real gaps are narrow, concentrated, and sit entirely inside its own stated mission — evidence sufficiency, authority currency, and a missing composite admissibility verdict, all of which are natural next steps for a Continuity and Intelligence platform, not signs of an incomplete product. Nearly everything a conventional enterprise-readiness or runtime-governance lens would flag as "critical" — identity federation, runtime enforcement, multi-tenancy, AI-gateway execution interception — is, once checked against what OMG actually says it is, evidence that its architectural boundary is being held with unusual discipline.
The risk worth naming plainly: the temptation to chase Kari's and Szilvia's runtime-adjacent language into building execution interception, identity verification, or enforcement into OMG core. Every one of those would trade OMG's most defensible property — a clean, advisory, human-accountable boundary — for a capability that belongs, correctly, to the customer's own runtime environment or identity provider, delivered through ODF, not built into the product.
This assessment applies the critical rule throughout: absence was never treated as a gap until first checked against OMG's stated core, configuration, ODF, and customer-responsibility layers. No code was modified in the course of this review.
